# The Second Draft

**Date:** 26 August 2026
**Status:** directions approved; features approved in order

The thread replaced seven screens on 25 August and taught us three things it got
wrong. This records what was decided about them, and the four features chosen
off the back of it, so the decisions live somewhere that is not a chat log.

Three rounds of mockups sit behind it. What follows is only what survived.

---

## 1. Buddy loses his bubble

**Buddy's words sit directly on the field**, cream, in the casual axis, with the
face and nothing else. Only *your* words keep a bubble.

The reasoning is not that it looks quieter, though it does. It is that the
screen had two kinds of thing wearing one costume: sentences and objects were
both cream rectangles with a 3px outline. Taking the bubble off Buddy means
**every remaining rectangle is a thing you can act on** — a note, an
appointment, a chore, or something you said. The same conversation goes from
seven outlined boxes to three.

What it costs is the chat convention. Buddy's words no longer read as
"received", which is a real loss for somebody arriving for the first time —
and is why the first-run screen below exists.

## 2. Six kinds of thing, six bodies

A note, a task, a chore, an appointment, a photograph and something set aside
were one card with different words. They become different objects, using
devices the system already owns:

| Kind | Body |
| --- | --- |
| note | plain stock, sitting slightly askew |
| task | stock wearing the notebook's page tab |
| chore | stock carrying its rhythm as a bar |
| appointment | a **ticket** — notched, time large in Inter, tear-off stub for what to bring |
| photograph | a polaroid |
| set aside | recessed and dashed rather than raised: present, but not a thing you can pick up |

The appointment is the one that justifies the work on its own. It is the only
thing in this product you can be *late* for, and it currently looks exactly
like a note about a rattle.

**Set-aside breaks "cream card stock, never white"** by not being stock at all.
Deliberate, and the one rule bent here.

## 3. The frame goes

The rail of four doors, the always-on chip row and the `stop whenever you like`
line all come off. They were about 45% of a phone screen before a word was said.

**The doors get two homes, not one.** Buddy mentions a place when there is
something in it — that is good copy, and it belongs in a sentence. A menu in
the lid holds every destination permanently — that is navigation, and it must
not scroll away. Doing only the first makes the product unnavigable ten turns
in; doing only the second loses the reason Buddy is there.

## 4. What's coming, first

The most common reason to open Squirrel is to see what is coming, and it was
three interactions deep behind a door press while the conversation opened with
history.

**Buddy opens with it**, as a turn. Not a standing strip: a fixture that
rewrites itself is a record that rewrites itself.

**Counts stay**, and countdowns are allowed where a number is a fact rather
than a score — a departure, an elapsed wait. Rule 2 still bans counting what
remains of *your work*; it does not ban saying that a thing will happen to you
at half past two.

## 5. Triage: four verbs, and a question

Seven equally-weighted buttons is six too many on a screen whose premise is
that deciding is the expensive part.

**Four filled buttons end the card** — done, keep, drop, a task — in the four
state colours. Everything else moves behind one ghost chip, `something else?`,
which answers **as a turn** rather than as a panel expanding: the card above
dims and keeps its place, and Buddy replies with the questions. Room appears
there for `break it up`, which today only shows when a free check guesses it is
worth offering.

## 6. Search results open into a card

A hit is not a card. A card is something you act on; a hit is something you are
finding, and making them the same object is why search felt like a second pile.

Results are quiet lines with the match underlined in orange. **One at a time
opens into a real card** with real buttons — the same one-thing-is-live rule the
thread already lives by. Six hits fit where three cards did.

`that is all of them` at the end. Search is the one place this product can
honestly say a list is complete.

## 7. The readings are a calendar

Six weeks by seven days, in the four mood colours, with **days you said nothing
drawn as dotted outlines**.

The gaps are the honest part and the reason this shape won. You check in on some
days and not others; a bar chart silently hides that, and a sentence has to
gloss it. A grid shows it.

No trend line, no average, no number. You read it; the product does not. It
stays reachable only by name, and nothing else in the product reads a mood
series — the same defence the spend counter uses.

Rejected: Buddy summarising it in a sentence. "mostly calm" is a small
judgement about a person, and it is still a judgement.

## 8. First run, for somebody who has never seen this

A demo visitor gets an empty pile and a box saying *put it down here*.

Instead Buddy plays a short **worked example** at reduced opacity — a capture,
an answer, a chore being offered — then rules a line under it and hands over.
The visitor sees the loop before being asked to trust it with anything, and
seeing a dimmed example of *your* bubble next to Buddy's unbubbled words teaches
the grammar of the screen in one look.

**The example is never stored as turns.** It is drawn, not remembered. The
first thing this product does must not be to write a false memory.

## The four features, in order

Chosen against the roadmap so that none was already decided.

### 1. Keeping your place — *first*

You start triaging, the phone rings, and forty minutes later Buddy has
forgotten you were doing anything. **Losing your place is the failure this
entire product is built around**, and there is no memory of a run in progress.

A run remembers where it got to. Coming back, Buddy says so before anything
else and offers both honest answers: **carry on** or **start fresh**.

**Expiry is the design.** A run older than a few hours is not offered at all.
Coming back to yesterday's half-finished pile is not resuming, it is being
nagged — and that is the rule to hold hardest if anything here is ever relaxed.

"2 of 5 left" is a count of a thing *you started*, which is the strongest form
of the number Rule 2 allows.

Needs no model, no new screen and no notification.

### 2. Rhythms that match real life

Chores are *every N days* and almost nothing real is. Bins are alternating
Thursdays; rent is the first of the month; a boiler service is annual and
drifts.

Four named rhythms: **weekly**, **alternating weekday**, **day of month**, and
**when i say** — the last being a chore with no schedule at all, which is the
honest shape for the ones you cannot predict.

Smallest useful slice is alternating-weekday alone.

### 3. Waiting, that speaks up

The three set-aside states shipped in August as a **one-way door**. You park
something waiting on the surgery precisely so you do not have to hold it, which
only works if something else is holding it.

An old one comes back **once**, in the opening turn, with elapsed time. `still
waiting` is a first-class answer that costs nothing and resets the clock.

The threshold is a property of the state — three weeks is right for a referral
and absurd for a text message — and **someday never speaks up at all**.

### 4. The hyperfocus exit ramp — *last*

Opt-in at timer start. Says something **once**, when the time is long past, and
`leave me alone` ends it for the day.

Last because it is the only one of the four that can annoy you, and it should
land when everything else is calm. The line to hold: opt-in, once per day, and
never for a timer you did not start yourself.

## The letter that becomes a fixed point — local only

A photographed hospital letter is the archetypal thing that never gets
transcribed. Buddy proposing a fixed point from it is the most valuable idea in
the study.

**It is done with no OCR, no model and no image leaving the cluster.** Look for
a date-shaped string in the words typed beside the photograph, propose from
that, and say nothing when there is not one.

This catches `dentist 4 sept 9.40` and misses a scanned letter entirely. That
is the tier the house rule asks for before anything goes abroad, and the
expensive version is not ruled out — it is just not first.
