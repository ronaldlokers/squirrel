## Design Specificity Verdict

Authored, unevenly — and the unauthored half is growing.

Distinctive and uncopyable: the stamp → 1150ms hold → on-card undo; the total absence of counts held with real discipline across every screen; the two-voice typographic split (his words casual, machinery precise) applied without exception; dropped-brown deliberately muted so dropping does not look like failure; the `<details>` refusal grammar with the dashed escape chip as the system's only dashed object; and the copy, which is writing rather than UX strings.

Category-interchangeable: chores is a settings list; tasks is a card list with a composer; Buddy is the live-chat convention with a skin; search is a standard result list; home is an app-launcher grid. The floating bottom-right circle is Intercom's shape, and it ships the full mascot at 46px rather than the acorn — so DESIGN.md's stated justification for the component is unfulfilled in the markup.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs` over `internal/web/templates` + `internal/web/static` — **exit 0, zero findings**. Verified by redirecting stdout/stderr rather than trusting the terminal echo. Nothing to classify as a false positive. The detector is silent because these are Go templates with hand-written CSS: it has no rule for a grid receiving more children than it has columns, nor for a duplicate class three hundred lines apart. Every defect below came from measurement or from looking.

## Overall Impression

The emotional thesis is intact and the craft on the original surface is real. What has degraded is discipline in extension: four screens added since the pile are conventional layouts wearing the palette, and the card itself has quietly grown from four actions to eight decision points while its CSS still says three.

The single biggest opportunity: **the card is the product, and it is the most broken thing in it.**

## What's Working

1. **The stamp / hold / undo mechanic.** The 1150ms hold exists so undo has somewhere to live, and that reasoning is legible in the artifact. It turns "did I just lose that?" into a beat where the answer is on the card.
2. **No-count discipline held under pressure.** Both assessments hunted for a leak and found none — not even on `held`, three sections of stalled work, the most tempting place in any product to print a number. The one allowed exception is quarantined to Buddy's lid with its reasoning in the markup.
3. **The `<details>` refusal grammar.** Reword, interval, can't-act, can't-start and new-chore all use one disclosure shape. Every "I can't" is cheap to reach and free to close.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The card with a photograph is broken, and it shipped three hours ago
`.note { display: flex; align-items: center }` makes `<details class="reword">`, `<p class="photo">` and `<p id="noteText">` horizontal siblings. A note with a photo puts the picture *beside* the words at ~70px wide on a phone — directly contradicting `card.html:32`'s own comment ("Above the words, because when there is a photograph the photograph is the note"). "fix the words" stacks as three lines inside the reading line. **Why it matters:** the case for the camera is photographing a letter instead of typing it. At 70px a letter is unreadable, so the feature does not deliver its own premise. **Fix:** `.note { flex-direction: column; align-items: flex-start }` and move the reword summary into the tray.

### [P0] The action row is a 3-column grid with 5 children
`pile.css:1411` sets `grid-template-columns: repeat(3, 1fr)`; the card has four answers plus `MAKE A CHORE`. `A TASK` lands alone in a one-third cell with its label wrapped to two lines while its siblings sit on one. The comment directly above claims "The five share the row exactly" — it was written when there were three, and `A TASK` was added without updating the grid. PRODUCT.md still says "Four actions per note". **Fix:** `repeat(2, 1fr)` so the four answers form a 2×2 and `.btn.make` spans beneath. Four equal answers, one creation, one row of escapes.

### [P1] The colour that means "done" also means "start a timer"
`.tbtn` is defined twice: `pile.css:830` gives `background: var(--paper)` for the chores timer row, with a comment explaining that intent; `pile.css:1020` redefines it as `--done-lifted` green for tasks. Identical specificity, later wins. Every chore shows DID IT in green followed by 5/10/20 MIN in the same green — four green pills, three of which start a timer. DESIGN.md explicitly reasons that a timer must not be orange "because orange means something was made and a timer makes nothing"; the same argument applies to green and the shipped screen contradicts it. **Fix:** scope the tasks rule to `.tacts .tbtn`. One line.

### [P1] Secondary type on purple fails AA nearly everywhere, worst on the error
Measured against the lit centre of the field's radial: `.hint` 3.49:1, `.more` 3.27:1, `.noted .again` 3.27:1, `.slotsaid` 3.49:1, and `.slotsaid.bad` — the message that tells you a capture failed — at **2.43:1**. `.noHits`, `.empty p`, `.ends a` sit at 4.19:1, marginal fails. The affected strings include the three check-in links on the low-capacity screen and **"stop whenever you like"**, the sentence carrying the product's third principle. **Fix:** full `--cream` for secondary type on purple; carry "quiet" with weight and size, not opacity. Reserve opacity for hover. Give `.slotsaid.bad` an opaque treatment that clears 4.5:1.

### [P1] The floating acorn sits on top of live controls
`.askacorn` is `position: fixed`, 62px; `main` carries only `padding-bottom: 32px` on phone. It covers the `20 MIN` pill on `phone-chores` and the corner of the SOMEDAY card on `phone-held`. A mis-tap opens a chat — the worst outcome for someone already low on capacity. **Fix:** `padding-bottom: calc(62px + 24px + env(safe-area-inset-bottom))` on `main`.

### [P2] The chores screen is 23 options; moods is unreadable
Chores: three chores × six controls, plus five more. The product's own accessibility section says decision load is the scarce resource. **Fix:** collapse the timer row behind the same `<details>` grammar the chore already uses, so a resting chore shows two controls and one escape. Moods: readings render as ~30px `<img>` with the word only in `alt`/`title`, unreachable on touch; and `elsewhere()` has no map entry for `moods`, so its lid renders three cross-links where every other screen gets two. **Fix:** print the word beside each face; add the missing map entries and make the fallback two links.

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (one thumb, interrupted):** acorn press is silent on a slow network then navigates away from the screen the conversation was about; acorn overlays the exact controls a thumb reaches for; `.rbtn` is 42px on mobile and `.find` ~38px, both under the 44px the project's own docs set; `A TASK`'s wrapped label makes the fastest one-thumb read the wrong one. *Credit:* draft survives closing Buddy, the slot keeps words on a failed write, offline capture says "I have it" — interruption recovery is the best-handled thing in the product.

**Sam (keyboard, screen reader, 200% zoom):** Buddy's transcript has no speaker attribution — `.mine` and `.his` differ only by alignment and background, so a screen reader gets an undifferentiated run of paragraphs, and `announce("Squirrel answered")` says a reply happened but never reads it; no `<h1>` on ten templates including pile, home, kept, results and coach; `.deckhead` has no CSS rule at all in 1907 lines; `.reword[open] ~ #noteText { display: none }` removes the note from the accessibility tree with no announcement. *Credit:* every `<img>` has `alt`, every form control has a label or `aria-label`, every icon-only control has an accessible name, and the native `<dialog showModal()>` buys Escape and a focus trap correctly.

**Riley (edge cases):** `main { display: flex; align-items: center }` with `.card { min-height: 296px }` puts a long note's top above the scroll origin — unreachable, and `.scrolling` is never set for the deck; the new-chore form is duplicated verbatim in `chores.html` and the two copies have already drifted (only one carries the hint); any search deletes `after=`, so search-then-refresh loses your place in the pile; `elsewhere()`'s fallback returns three links for any unmapped route, so the next screen added inherits the moods bug by default.

**Ronald on a bad day (23:00, low executive function):** asked "how do you feel?" before anything else — a self-assessment toll at the moment self-assessment is the expensive thing; opens the pile for one card and meets eight controls with one visibly wrapped; `NOTED — CALM` is a small sticker, an uppercase machine-voice label and three interchangeable low-contrast links; chores is 18 buttons; "stop whenever you like" is the faintest text on the screen at the hour it matters most. What holds him: the stamp, PUT IT BACK, and that nothing counts. Those three are doing the entire job.

## Minor Observations

- PRODUCT.md is stale in two places: it still says capture never happens on this screen ("permanent rather than a current limitation") and still says "Four actions per note".
- The acorn button is not an acorn — it renders `logo.png` at 46px, where the mascot's face is illegible mush. `card.html` contains an actual acorn SVG that could be lifted.
- `.his` is the class for Buddy's messages, in a product that named the character Buddy.
- `.ends` is defined twice; `.ends a` and `.ends a:hover` are byte-identical duplicates.
- Two empty-state grammars: pile/tasks/chores/archive get mascot + `<h1>` + sentence; held/moods/kept get a left-aligned line with no art.
- Deck and search disagree about one note: an open note offers 8 controls in the deck and 4 in search.
- The wide breakpoint is unconsidered — `desktop-moods` spends 1280×900 on three 30px icons.
- `role="status"` on statically-rendered elements: nothing to announce on first paint.

## Questions to Consider

1. The card has five answers and three escapes. What if it had one control — "what is this?" — and the answers appeared only after you pressed it? The decision becomes *am I deciding about this right now*, which is the decision that actually stalls.
2. `A TASK` and `MAKE A CHORE` both promote a note out of the pile. Is "once" vs "again" worth two buttons, or is it one button and a second question — the shape the interval picker already is?
3. What if the check-in were never on home — asked once by Squirrel in the room, where the product already speaks first — so the screen he opens when overwhelmed never begins with a question about himself?
4. Does "nothing accrues that can be destroyed" extend to *effort*? The check-in already drops two picker rules on a low reading. Why does it not also drop controls?
5. Habituation is named as a documented risk and the answer is 1.1° of stack rotation. Is that the whole budget?
6. What is the chores screen for, now that the picker exists? It is the one surface still organised by kind rather than by now.
7. What is the ending? The product has beautiful endings almost nobody reaches and none for the session that actually happens: closing the tab three notes in.
