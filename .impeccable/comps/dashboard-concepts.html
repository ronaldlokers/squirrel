Three ways this could stop being a conversation

Each one keeps the pile, the five rooms, the picker's six rules and the way a note is answered. What changes is the container it all sits in.

Sample words are authored. None of them are yours.

One — the desk

The conversation stops being the product's shape. Every room is a column of the things themselves, and you answer a note where it stands.

Keeps
The pile, the four answers, the six rules, the dock, the mark. Chat stays as the room in Campfire, which is where it always worked.
Costs
Everything shipped between v0.49 and v0.55: turns, rooms as threads, the check-in as a turn, how you felt before as a press. A note stops carrying when it was said.
Buddy
A standing column on the right. Present without being the container.

On a thumb

The columns become one column and the room names become the tabs above it, so the thing you are looking at is always named. The offer stays first, because on a phone you are usually here for one thing.

Buddy is the acorn, a press, not a column. There is no room on this screen for a conversation that is always visible, and pretending otherwise is how the desk would break on the sofa.

Two — the landing

One screen you arrive on and mostly look at. The five rooms stay conversations; this is the place that tells you which one to walk into.

Keeps
Everything. Not one turn is retired. The rooms are untouched behind their doors, and the phone gets the same screen rather than a lesser one.
Costs
One more press between you and the words. And a screen whose only job is to be looked at can become a screen you tidy.
Buddy
A strip along the bottom that is already talking. Pressing it opens the conversation you were half in.

On a thumb

The same screen, stacked in the order you need it: what Squirrel picked, what is running, what is coming, then the doors with what is behind them.

This is the only one of the three whose phone view is the same idea as its desktop view. Nothing is desktop-only, so nothing has to be re-learned when you sit down.

Three — the standing panel

The rail you already have stops being a list of doors and becomes a column that answers what is going on. The conversation is untouched beside it, and the phone never sees any of it.

Keeps
Every turn, every room, the dock, the phone exactly as it shipped last night. Two views, one pile: the rule the product already lives by.
Costs
The scattered feeling only lifts at the desk. On the sofa it is the same product it is today.
Buddy
The left pane is Buddy. He is always present because he never left.
